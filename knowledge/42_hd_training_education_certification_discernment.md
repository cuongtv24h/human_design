# Human Design Training: Education, Certification, and Discernment

If you’ve had a Human Design Foundation Reading and felt a genuine resonance with the system, it’s natural to become curious about how it works and how deep the knowledge goes. Some people are drawn to Human Design for personal understanding and self-observation. Others feel a pull to study it more formally or eventually work with others. Many move between these impulses over time.

This article outlines the general landscape of formal Human Design education, describes the main training paths that exist, and shares a few reflections drawn from experience. My hope is that it helps you situate yourself more clearly, wherever you are in your relationship with the system.

##### **Beginning with the Foundations**

Human Design is a precise system, but it is not absorbed all at once. Like learning a language, it unfolds through repetition, practice, and lived experience. The quality of guidance matters, but so does time.

In the early years of the system’s transmission, Ra Uru Hu offered teachings in short, intensive formats. Over time, it became clear that people needed more space to experiment, integrate, and live their design before moving deeper into the knowledge. This led to the formation of the International Human Design School (IHDS), which organized the material into a staged educational structure.

Within that structure, formal education begins with the _Foundation Courses_, alongside a personal Foundation Reading.

The first of these courses is **Living Your Design**. Rather than teaching people how to read charts professionally, this course introduces the essential mechanics of the system as they apply to everyday life. It covers core concepts such as Type, Strategy, Authority, and the Centers, with an emphasis on observing how these dynamics show up in lived experience.

Living Your Design is offered in a variety of formats, depending on the teacher: live classes, intensives, hybrid formats combining recordings with live sessions, and private mentorship. While the structure varies, the intention is the same. It establishes a baseline understanding of the system and invites an ongoing experiment with awareness.

If the Living Your Design course is taught well, it illuminates deeply ingrained, conditioned thought patterns that drive many common compulsive behaviors. The goal is to help the student recognize alternatives to these habituated ways of moving through life so that greater alignment and self-acceptance become possible.

##### **Deepening into the Mechanics**

After completing Living Your Design and receiving a Foundation Reading, some students feel ready to go further. The next course in the formal sequence is **Rave ABCs**.

Rave ABCs shifts attention toward the mechanics that underlie the BodyGraph. It explores the distinction between Design and Personality, introduces channel and gate circuitry, and lays out the structure of the hexagrams. This course often marks the point at which students begin to see how individual experience fits within a larger mechanical framework.

Most importantly, students often discover why they tend to hold certain priorities in how they approach life. This can also reveal the underlying mechanisms that account for differences in how individuals orient to relationships. Most students are amazed at how this course helps them understand themselves and the people in their lives.

From there, **Rave Cartography** completes the Foundation-level education. This course offers an in-depth exploration of Profile, types of Definition, the Mandala (the astrological wheel including the signs of the zodiac and the hexagrams of the I Ching), Gates, Channels, and Authority, integrating these elements more cohesively. It provides the conceptual grounding needed to recognize nuanced chart mechanics, whether for personal understanding or future professional work.

Together, Living Your Design, Rave ABCs, and Rave Cartography form a coherent foundation. For many people, completing these three courses takes 6 months to 1 year or more, depending on pace and level of integration. They are not meant to be rushed.

One of my primary teachers, Alokanand Diaz, framed these courses not primarily as professional prerequisites but as general education on what it means to be a nine-centered human living at this time. That perspective didn’t fully land for me at first, but over time I’ve come to see its accuracy.

##### **Formal Training and Certification Paths**

After completing the Foundation Courses, the educational landscape opens up in several directions. Which path is appropriate, if any, depends ideally on what feels most true for you. For some, the desire is simply to learn more, or there is an inner sense that they should continue their exploration. It may also depend on timing, temperament, and intention.

One option is the **Living Your Design Guide** training. This program prepares people to host and teach the Living Your Design workshop, focusing on clearly and responsibly communicating the foundational mechanics. Its purpose is not to qualify someone to offer professional chart readings, but rather to share the most basic practical aspects of Human Design with newcomers.

For those interested in becoming professional Human Design analysts, the **Professional Analyst Training Program (PTL)** offers a longer, more demanding path. This training typically spans two years or more and covers partnership analysis, life cycles, Incarnation Crosses, and advanced keynoting, culminating in certification to offer professional readings as an IHDS-certified analyst.

There is also a parallel educational track at the **BG5 Institute**, an arm of the IHDS, that applies Human Design principles to career and business contexts. BG5 uses different language and keynotes to describe similar Human Design concepts, and focuses on organizational dynamics, leadership, and group mechanics rather than individual life readings.

Beyond these primary routes, additional advanced programs explore health, psychology, family dynamics, cosmology, and other specialized applications of the system. Some are prerequisites for teaching within the formal educational structure itself.

##### **Lineage, Adaptation, and Discernment**

Human Design has now been in the world for several decades, and with time comes variation. Some teachers who trained directly with Ra Uru Hu or within the IHDS system have gone on to develop their own language, emphasis, or certification structures. Others teach Human Design alongside other systems, or outside any formal lineage.

This diversity is not inherently problematic. Systems evolve. Language shifts. In most teaching lineages, some first-generation students separate from the original teacher to create their own version of the work.

At the same time, Human Design has a specific internal logic. When that logic is substantially altered, a different system is being taught, even if the name remains the same. In those cases, clarity matters. So, it helps to know whether you are studying Human Design as transmitted or a derivative system inspired by it.

Certification can provide one form of legitimacy, but it is not the sole indicator of depth or integrity. Some individuals without formal credentials have developed a refined understanding through years of study, observation, and lived experimentation. Others may hold certifications yet teach prematurely, without sufficient integration.

While formal certification can indicate that the practitioner at least met the certifying organization’s requirements and give others a general idea of what to expect from a consultation, there is no single metric that fully resolves this tension.

Luckily, many practitioners and teachers have materials, podcasts, videos, or audio samples that can give you an opportunity to experience their energy, voice, and style, and to see whether you feel drawn to the way they transmit information and their understanding of it.

##### **A Few Closing Thoughts**

If you are considering formal Human Design training, my general encouragement is simple. Slow down. Let your relationship with the system mature. Pay attention to why you feel drawn to study, teaching, or certification, and notice how those impulses evolve as you continue experimenting with your design.

Formal education can be deeply supportive when it arrives at the right moment. It can also be constraining when pursued too early or for the wrong reasons. Both outcomes are evident in lived experience.

At the Human Design Collective, we work with people at many stages of engagement. Some are just beginning. Some are studying deeply. Some move in and out of formal education over time. Our role is not to decide that for anyone but to offer clear information, grounded guidance, and a space where discernment is respected.

If you feel drawn to explore further, trust your curiosity. If you feel unsure, trust that too.
